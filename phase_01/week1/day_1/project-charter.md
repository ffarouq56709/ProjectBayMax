## Project purpose

Design and build a life-size Baymax-style robot that explores how robotics can support caregivers and help people through friendly interaction, health-data collection, secure notifications and controlled object handling.

The project is a research prototype. It will support—not replace—caregivers and medical professionals.

## One-year required demonstration

By the end of the first year, the robot shall:

1. Stand approximately 1,829 mm tall.
2. Complete three consecutive slow steps twice while attached to approved fall protection.
3. Collect simulated or consented health readings from approved external sensors.
4. Display the readings with the measurement time and sensor source.
5. Recognize a scripted concerning event using predefined rules.
6. Ask the person whether assistance is needed.
7. Send a structured test report to an approved caregiver or medical-team test account.
8. Lift a 2 kg test object while stationary and mechanically supported.
9. Protect collected health information from unauthorized access.
10. Clearly report its limitations and never claim to have diagnosed the person.

## Detailed requirements

### Physical platform

The robot shall be 1,829 ±10 mm tall.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Measure the CAD model and completed robot.

The robot shall target a mass of 55 kg or less, excluding external test equipment.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: CAD mass report followed by measurement using a scale.

The lower body shall contain 10 active joints: hip pitch, hip roll, knee pitch, ankle pitch and ankle roll on each leg.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Inspect the joint table, CAD assembly and physical robot.

The robot shall complete two successful sequences of three consecutive tethered steps at no more than 0.15 m/s.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Synchronized video and telemetry from a supervised test operated by an authorized test team.

### Health-data collection

The robot shall receive heart-rate, oxygen-saturation, temperature or activity information from commercial external sensors rather than attempting to create unvalidated medical measurements internally.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Record the sensor identifier, timestamp, units and received value during a test.

The first demonstration shall use simulated data or data from a consenting adult participant.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Review the test-data declaration and consent record.

The robot shall label every reading with:

- Measurement type
- Numerical value
- Units
- Sensor source
- Date and time
- Whether the data is simulated or real
- Whether the reading was successfully transmitted

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Inspect the stored and displayed test record.

### Caregiver alert

The robot shall detect a predefined test condition, such as:

- A simulated fall
- A spoken request for help
- Failure to respond to two prompts
- A simulated measurement outside a clinician-provided test range

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Run one test for every alert condition and record the result.

The robot shall describe what it observed without announcing a diagnosis.

Acceptable message:

“Possible fall detected at 2:14 PM. The person did not respond to two prompts.”

Unacceptable message:

“The patient has suffered a medical emergency.”

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Review every generated message against the approved wording.

The robot shall send the test report to an approved test recipient within 60 seconds of the confirmed alert.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Compare the alert timestamp with the received-message timestamp.

The report shall contain:

- Robot identifier
- Event type
- Date and time
- Sensor readings
- Response to verbal prompts
- Robot location within the test area
- Actions already taken
- Data-quality warnings
- A statement that the report was generated by a prototype

### Privacy and consent

The robot shall obtain recorded consent before collecting or transmitting identifiable health information.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Confirm that a consent record exists before enabling data collection.

The robot shall send information only to approved recipients through an encrypted connection.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Inspect the recipient list, transmission configuration and security-test results.

The robot shall maintain an audit record containing:

- What information was collected
- When it was collected
- Who received it
- When it was transmitted
- Whether transmission succeeded
- Who changed the recipient list

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Generate and review the audit report after every demonstration.

Real patient information shall not be used until Jacobs and the participating medical organization approve the research, privacy, security and data-handling procedures.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Written approval is present before real patient information is enabled.

### Object handling

The required first-year payload shall be 2 kg.

The robot shall lift the test object:

- While stationary
- While mechanically supported
- From approximately 500 mm to 1,000 mm above the floor
- With the load kept close to the torso
- Inside a cleared test area
- Without a person beneath or beside the load

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Complete 10 supervised repetitions without dropping the object, exceeding limits or permanently deforming the structure.

The stretch payload shall be 5 kg. Testing it requires a separate structural review, arm-actuator review and approved test procedure.

The robot shall not walk while carrying the test payload during the first-year demonstration.

The robot shall not lift, catch, transfer or physically support a person.

### Failure behavior

If power, communications, sensor data or software control is lost, the robot shall:

1. Stop intentional movement.
2. Retain or safely lower the test object according to the reviewed mechanism.
3. Enter its defined safe state.
4. Record the fault.
5. Notify the test supervisor when communications remain available.

Owner: Faisal Farouq  
Verification: Conduct supervised fault-injection tests with an authorized test team.

## Project priorities

Priority 1: Safe walking platform  
Priority 2: Health-data collection and caregiver reporting  
Priority 3: Stationary 2 kg object-handling demonstration  
Stretch goal: Stationary 5 kg object handling  
Future work: Clinical deployment, real medical-system integration or person-handling capabilities  

If the budget or schedule cannot support all requirements, the project shall protect the higher priorities first.

## Excluded first-year claims

The first-year prototype shall not claim that it:

- Diagnoses diseases or medical emergencies
- Recommends treatments or medication changes
- Replaces a caregiver, nurse, physician or emergency service
- Guarantees that an alert reaches or is reviewed by medical staff
- Safely lifts or physically supports people
- Operates untethered around the public
- Walks while carrying a heavy payload
